# Interfacial Passivation of Perovskite Solar Cells with Phenethylammonium Iodide

Abstract: Interfacial passivation of perovskite solar cells has attracted growing attention in perovskite photovoltaics. In this work, we investigate phenethylammonium iodide (PEAI) treatment on MAPbI3 films. The results show that the treated devices reach a power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 23.5% under 1 sun illumination, with an open-circuit voltage of 1.12 V and a short-circuit current density of 23.5 mA cm-2 [12]. The unencapsulated devices retains 78% of their initial efficiency after 1000 h at 45% relative humidity. These findings proves that PEAI passivation is the best strategy for stable PSC.

## 1. Introduction

Metal halide perovskites have attracted increasing attention because of their tunable bandgap and long carrier diffusion length [3,4]. Perovskite solar cells (PSC) have reached certified efficiencies above 26%, yet interfacial defects limit both efficiency and stability. Interfacial defects are important in non-radiative recombination, however, the mechanisms of passivation remain debated (Smith et al., 2021). To suppress these defects, several groups compared ammonium salts. PEAI was reported to passivate the surface of PSC, but the reported stability gains are inconsistent across laboratories.

## 2. Methods

MAPbI3 precursor solutions were prepared at 2.5 mg mL-1 in dimethylformamide. Films were deposited by spin-coating at 3000 rpm for 30 s, then annealed at 150 C for 10 min. PEAI was dissolved in isopropanol and spin-coated onto the cooled films. Control samples were prepared by spin-coating without PEAI. X-ray diffraction (XRD) patterns were collected on a diffractometer using Cu K-alpha radiation.

Absorption spectra were recorded from 300 nm to 800 nm. The film thickness was determined by a stylus profilometer, as described in Section 2.1. Steady-state photoluminescence was excited at 532 nm and the decay was fitted with a bi-exponential function, see Eq. (4).

## 3. Results

Figure 2 shows the XRD patterns of the control and PEAI-treated films. The (110) reflection at 14.1 degrees is unchanged, while the PbI2 peak at 12.7 degrees decreases after treatment. The results indicate that unreacted PbI2 is converted into the perovskite phase,these observations are consistent with a surface reaction.

The treated films exhibit a longer carrier lifetime (Table 1). The average PCE increases from 20.1% to 23.5%, and the hysteresis index decreases from 0.08 to 0.02. The champion device retains 78% of its initial efficiency after 1000 h at 85% relative humidity, which suggests that the passivation layer slow down moisture ingress.

## 4. Discussion

The improvement is attributed to defect passivation, since the PL lifetime increases by a factor of 3. The PEAI layer reduces surface defects. We note that the stability trend may depend on the encapsulation strategy, and the mechanism is not fully resolved. Our data indicate that the passivation is partly reversible at elevated temperature.

Several limitations should be considered. Devices were measured under 45% relative humidity, whereas field conditions vary widely. The absorption edge at 780 nm is unchanged, which indicates that the treatment does not alter the bandgap. Long-term operation data are not available in this work.

## 5. Conclusion

We show that PEAI passivation improves both efficiency and moisture stability of MAPbI3 devices. The treated devices are more stable than the controls under the conditions tested here. Future work will examine whether the same treatment is effective for wider-bandgap compositions.
